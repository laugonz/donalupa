Is Doña Lupa really sudoku?

It uses sudoku's one-per-row, one-per-column and one-per-zone rule, but replaces numbers with illustrated suspects and adds truthful story clues. You never need to calculate anything.

Learn how deduction sudoku works →
Do I need to know number sudoku first?

No. The interactive first case teaches rows, columns, zones, notes and clues as you use them.

Read the beginner guide →
What are the basic deduction sudoku rules?

Place every suspect once in each row, column and outlined zone. Combine those constraints with the case clues until every cell is determined.

See the rules step by step →
Are all the clues true?

Yes. Every clue in a Doña Lupa case is true, so a contradiction means an earlier placement needs another look.

Learn how to cross clues →
Do I have to guess when I get stuck?

No. Keep both candidates open, inspect their intersecting row, column and zone, and return to clues that gained meaning after your last placement.

Are the mysteries scary or violent?

No. The cases are cozy comic mysteries about missing lunches, suspicious coffee and other very small crimes.

Does every case have one solution?

Yes. The clues and board constraints are designed to lead to one checkable final arrangement.
